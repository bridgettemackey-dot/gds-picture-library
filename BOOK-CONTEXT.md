## THE GIRL AND THE WHALE (reader)

Story by Zhané Mackey. GDS Reading Series, Level 3.

**Audience:** market it as ages 6–12, grades 1–6, which is what the activity book's
own title page says and what Bridge confirmed on 2026-09-14. The reader's title page
says "Level 3 Reader | Grades 2-3 | Ages 7-9" — that is the outlier, do not quote it.

### People

| Who | What the book says |
|---|---|
| **Anna Lelani Mahoe** | The full name, used by her mother when she is in trouble. "Chocolate-brown skin. Her hair was darker than the stripes on a zebra." Depicted 12–15 (standing rule). |
| **Mr. Hooker** | "One of the greatest fishermen in Hawaii", knows many island stories. Tells Anna the legend and gives her the book. |
| **Kelani** | Anna's mother. Sceptical — "You do not think anything is real," Gran Gran tells her. |
| **Gran Gran** | Kelani's mother. Covers for Anna with the mahi-mahi story. |
| **The Cosmic Theseus Finback** | FEMALE — "her fins glowed a cosmic purple". The largest humpback in the world. Took a comet strike to save every sea creature. Her fins "shone brighter than all the stars in the sky combined". |

### The story, in order

1. Anna hears whale song on the beach and follows it to the docks.
2. The docks are empty. Mr. Hooker says she is hallucinating, then mentions the legend.
3. He tells her the Cosmic Finback story and gives her **Hawaii's Book of Legends**.
4. Instruction: do the dance **by the rocks at the edge of the coral reef, at sunset**.
5. Kelani questions where she has been; Gran Gran covers for her.
6. Anna dances at the reef as the sun goes down — "the sky glowed coral and gold".
   Nothing happens. She gives up.
7. A purple glow. The whale appears. Anna screams and falls backwards.
8. The whale lifts her onto a glowing fin and carries her "above the mountains,
   beaches, houses, and sea". The people of Hawaii look up and see her.
9. Kelani and Gran Gran see the light from the window.
10. The whale lands her, then "turned into stardust and floated into the sky".
11. Kelani shouts her full name. Anna admits she wanted her mother to be proud.
    The three of them gaze at the stars.

**Lesson:** "If you believe in something enough, it will come true!"

## GOING TO THE REGATTA (reader)

By Bridgette Skeete-Mackey. GDS Reading Series, Family Reader, ages 5–15.
A TRUE family account illustrated with REAL FAMILY PHOTOGRAPHS.

> **NEVER extract an image from this book.** Nicholas is photographed directly;
> Nicole and Zhané appear in the gallery pages. Use generated illustration and the
> one catalogued colouring page only. This is Rule 1 and it has no exceptions.

### The family
Nicholas · his mother **Nicole** · his sister **Zhané** · Aunties Sharon, Anna and
Angela · **Uncle Dennis** (lives in Boynton Beach, Florida) · cousins **Anthony** and
**Rashaan** · **Aunty Monique "Aunty Nickie"**, 60, Chief Social Worker on Long
Island — she and Nicholas share a nickname, so the family calls them **Big Nick and
Little Nick**. **Aunty Paula** lived in Deadman's Cay with her husband and three
daughters; she did not come on this trip. Grandmother **Dourilease Gray-Skeete**,
raised in Simms by **Typheus** (farmer and fisherman) and **Edla** (homemaker).
Uncle Chesley and Uncle Dennis and Aunty Sharon were born and raised on Long Island.

### The five days
- **Planning.** Tickets bought in January; June flights from Nassau fill fast. Some
  travellers fly to Exuma and continue by ferry.
- **Thursday.** Land at Stella Maris, North Long Island. Four-bedroom house
  overlooking the Atlantic; a rocky path past the backyard leads to the **nature
  bath**, a clear natural pool. Lunch at **Green Leaf**, run by a husband-and-wife
  team. Then the long drive to **Salt Pond** in Central Long Island: food vendors,
  craft and merchandise stalls, racing sloops, private boats carrying spectators,
  Bahamian artists on stage in the evening.
- **Friday.** Nicole and her sisters walk Stella Maris at dawn. Friday soup at Green
  Leaf and a freshly opened coconut. A cousin runs a **hoop-la table** at the
  regatta; Nicholas wins nothing.
- **Saturday.** The nature bath before breakfast — Auntie Sharon stays near the steps,
  unsure about going further. Then **chicken souse** with pepper, lime and allspice,
  and **johnny cake**, "crisp outside, soft inside, and spread with butter". The pan
  is empty before breakfast ends. Final regatta night: results and prizes, music and
  dancing. The drive home after midnight with the fuel gauge dropping — they reach
  the house **with one bar remaining**.
- **Sunday.** Worship at **Beulah Baptist Church, Simms**. Dinner: peas and rice,
  baked chicken, fried fish, plantains, potato salad. Dessert planned, nobody had room.
- **Monday.** Home from **Stella Maris airport** — the only one operating, because
  Deadman's Cay airport was being renovated.

### Regatta history
First recorded organised race at **Deadman's Cay, 1868**. Racing near Salt Pond from
**1967**, when the schools inspector and others began racing; an American businessman
watching offered a trophy, and a three-day event followed. From **1969** Long Island
descendants in New Providence raised money for prizes. **August 1973**: the Long
Islanders Association formed, working on education, recreation, medical assistance
and support for Long Island families.

**Lesson:** "A regatta is more than a race. It is a homecoming where history, family,
and island life meet."

### THE SLOOP — GET THIS RIGHT
The book teaches the **C1 class**: **one large mainsail and NO JIB**, one mast, boom,
hull, bow. A generated "Bahamian sloop" came back as a two-masted yacht on 2026-09-06.
Say explicitly: one mast set well forward, one enormous triangular mainsail, no
second sail, a boom running well past the stern, a shallow wooden hull, and a long
pry board out over the windward side. Then LOOK at what comes back.

## THE RULE THIS DOCUMENT EXISTS TO ENFORCE

On 2026-09-13 a caption described "plates being carried carefully" and "the person
turning round to call someone" on a page where nobody carries a plate and everyone is
posed facing forward. The alt text said "children carrying plates of food"; there is
one child and he is holding a flag. Both were invented from the filename
